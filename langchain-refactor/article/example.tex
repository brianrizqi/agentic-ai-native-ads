\begin{abstract}
Native advertisements are increasingly common as hybrid commercial content embedded within digital news platforms. Although these ads can enhance user engagement, they often elicit negative reactions due to their concealed commercial intent. A key challenge in detecting native advertisements lies in their lack of explicit labeling, which can mislead readers and compromise the perceived credibility of news content. Prior studies have struggled to accurately identify native ads because of their inability to capture the implicit characteristics embedded in editorial-like text, such as positive sentiment, persuasive language, one-sided perspectives, and references to specific products or companies. To address this issue, this study proposes a deep learning architecture called SENADA (Stacked Ensemble for Native Advertising Analysis). The model integrates BERT-based contextual representations, ensemble learning strategies, and a BiLSTM with an attention mechanism. This design enables the model to effectively identify subtle patterns and contextual cues associated with native advertisements. In addition, we introduce a newly constructed Indonesian-language dataset of news articles, annotated based on the four implicit characteristics of native ads. The dataset was collected from six major e-news portals and manually labeled by trained annotators. Experimental evaluations, based on confusion matrix analysis, demonstrate that SENADA achieves an accuracy of 0.93, along with high values for precision, recall, and F1-score. The model is compared against several well-established baselines, including CNN, BiLSTM, BiGRU, and transformer-based architectures, which were selected based on their widespread use and proven effectiveness in prior text classification studies. The proposed model consistently outperforms these baselines, particularly in minimizing misclassification of borderline content, defined as news articles that exhibit some but not all native ad characteristics, such as persuasive tone without explicit product mention or positive sentiment without clear promotional framing. These borderline cases were validated through manual annotation and cross-checking among annotators to ensure reliability. These results confirm the model’s effectiveness in detecting native advertisements that exhibit complex and implicit textual patterns. While the model shows promising results, future work is needed to reduce architectural complexity and training time. Further research could explore explainability methods and cross-lingual adaptation to extend the model’s applicability across languages and media contexts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
The term "native advertising" refers to advertising integrated into the editorial content of the electronic news portal in which it appears \cite{b1}. Although native advertising has been used extensively in traditional media, including magazines, newspapers, television and radio, technological advancements in digital media, including social media, have prompted marketers to view it as one of the most prominent digital tactics \cite{b2}. Native advertisements, which resemble the original content on various online platforms, including news, sports, and social media websites, are shared online advertising in the digital age \cite{b3}. A serious challenge is that native ads are increasingly difficult to distinguish from regular news content. This type of advertising is designed to provide a better user experience and usually appears as promoted content \cite{b4}. This phenomenon can affect the reader's perceptions of the truth of the information and threaten the credibility of the media.

The definition of native advertising itself differs across domains. In marketing, it refers to paid content intentionally designed to match the look and feel of the platform where it appears, aiming to engage users without disrupting their experience. Meanwhile, in journalism, native ads are often viewed more critically due to their potential to blur the boundary between editorial content and commercial messaging. Because these ads often resemble authentic news stories, they can undermine editorial integrity and mislead readers about the origin and purpose of the information. This dual interpretation makes native ads uniquely challenging to identify and regulate, especially in fast-paced digital news environments.

Native ads have headlines that resemble regular news, but are primarily favorable. Native ads articles also tend to have positive or neutral content and are usually only presented from one point of view \cite{b5}. According to Wojdynski \cite{b6}, native ads publishers have a financial incentive to portray a particular company or product favorably. In addition, the most common format in this case is native advertising, which presents persuasive messages under the guise of regular news, blurring the boundaries between editorial and advertising content \cite{b7}. In summary, native ads have four implicit characteristics that distinguish them from regular news: (1) positive or neutral sentiment, (2) persuasive, (3) favorably promoting a product or company, and (4) presenting a perspective from one point of view without involving opposing views. These four characteristics are often used to subtly influence readers without realizing they consume sponsored content. Identifying these characteristics manually is challenging due to the complexity and large volume of online news. These characteristics are subtle and difficult to detect manually, especially considering the large volume of news content and the nuanced language used.

In addition to the technical challenges, classifying editorial content as native advertising based on implicit linguistic cues raises ethical concerns. The concerns include the possibility of mislabeling, which may negatively affect the reputation of journalists or publishers if their content is mistakenly categorized as promotional. Since many native ads are not explicitly disclosed, distinguishing them from regular news can be highly subjective. This research does not seek to judge editorial intent but instead aims to promote transparency by identifying rhetorical and semantic patterns commonly found in native ads. Future work should involve media professionals in reviewing model predictions and developing clear ethical guidelines for real-world applications of such models.

Previous research has attempted to detect deceptive content in news, including fake news and propaganda, by applying various machine learning and deep learning approaches. However, these studies typically focus on explicit indicators and often rely on binary classification tasks. They rarely address the more subtle features found in native advertisements. Moreover, most prior works use English-language datasets and do not explore native ads detection in low-resource languages such as Indonesian. These gaps limit the generalizability and effectiveness of existing methods for native ad identification in diverse media contexts. To address these limitations, this study proposes a deep learning framework designed to capture the implicit characteristics of native ads using a combination of contextual representation and ensemble modeling. By leveraging pre-trained language models, ensemble learning, and attention mechanisms, the proposed method focuses on detecting nuanced signals in content that resembles editorial writing. This approach aims to move beyond simple classification and instead model native ads as texts that contain overlapping traits such as sentiment, persuasion, product mentions, and one-sided perspectives.

The BERT model served as the foundation for Fawaid's study \cite{b8}, which assessed the effectiveness of the model in detecting fake news in contrast to deep learning algorithms. The study results proved that the precision of BERT could reach 90\%, while BiLSTM was only 85\%. Vlad \cite{b9} detected propaganda sentences in news articles using the transfer learning model and the BERT-BiLSTM-Capsule model. This study's challenge was building a robust binary classifier that could provide appropriate propaganda or non-propaganda labels. The results showed that BERT-BiLSTM got the highest accuracy and recall values, namely 77\% and 85.5\%, while BERT-BiLSTM-Capsule got the highest precision and F1-score values, namely 85\% and 61.6\%. Sadeghi \cite{b10} proposed a new fake news detection method based on the NLI approach. The study used the NLI approach to improve several machine learning, deep learning models, and Word embedding methods. The results of BERT-BiLSTM get a high accuracy value of 90\% on the FNID-FakeNewsNet dataset and 39.6\% on the FNID-LIAR dataset. Based on the literature study above, deep learning is more effective than machine learning. Although these studies demonstrate the promise of combining BERT with sequential models such as BiLSTM and capsule networks, they do not focus on the implicit nature of native ads or the challenges of detecting such content in multilingual or low-resource environments. This study seeks to address those challenges by introducing a more targeted approach.

Deep learning is a subfield of machine learning inspired by brain function. One way to utilize deep learning is ensemble learning. Ensemble learning uses multiple algorithms to achieve better predictive performance than their constituent algorithms can, and some have used deep learning approaches as a means for ensemble learning \cite{b11}. In updating systematic reviews, Qin \cite{b12} investigated how well NLP technology works to speed up the title and abstract screening procedure. Qin used group learning strategies. Combining the strengths of four pre-trained BERT models, the classifier is a LightGBM ensemble model. The sensitivity value of the LightGBM ensemble is 95\%. Fattahi \cite{b13} proposed a new method called SpaML, which uses NLP techniques that present the Ensemble learning classifier. The results of this study prove that SpaML gets the highest accuracy and precision scores compared to other methods. However, these methods have not yet been applied to detect native ads, particularly in the Indonesian context.

The main contributions of this study are outlined as follows. First, it addresses the lack of research on native advertisement detection in low-resource languages, particularly Indonesian, where existing approaches are scarce and often limited to generic text classification methods. To close this gap, the study introduces a deep learning architecture named SENADA (Stacked Ensemble for Native Advertising Analysis). SENADA uniquely differs from conventional ensemble models by integrating feature-level representations instead of prediction-level outputs, enabling richer semantic modeling. It integrates ensemble learning, BERT-based feature extraction, and a BiLSTM model enhanced with an attention mechanism. The architecture is designed to identify native advertisements in digital news articles by capturing implicit linguistic and structural patterns. SENADA departs from conventional ensemble approaches that typically combine prediction outputs. Instead, it trains four independent deep learning components, each specialized in learning a specific native ad characteristic—positive sentiment, persuasive tone, product or company reference, and single narrative perspective. Each component produces a sentence-level vector that encodes task-specific semantic information. These vectors are then concatenated to form a unified representation, further processed by a classification module.

The choice of integrating BERT, ensemble learning, and BiLSTM with attention is motivated by the complementary strengths of these components and their relevance to the challenges of native advertisement detection. BERT is employed as the primary feature extractor because of its strong contextual encoding ability, which captures nuanced semantic and syntactic patterns in text that are essential for identifying subtle persuasive cues. While BERT provides powerful embeddings, it does not explicitly model sequential dependencies. To address this, BiLSTM is incorporated to capture bidirectional contextual flow across sentences, enabling the detection of narrative framing that often characterizes native ads. The attention mechanism is introduced to highlight the most influential words or phrases within a sentence, thereby enhancing interpretability and ensuring that critical cues, such as promotional mentions or persuasive markers, are not overshadowed by less relevant context. Finally, ensemble learning integrates multiple task-specific encoders, each focusing on one implicit characteristic of native ads, into a unified representation. This design is preferred over single end-to-end transformer models as it explicitly separates and preserves diverse semantic perspectives before final classification, resulting in both higher interpretability and improved performance.

This feature-level integration enables the model to retain and combine diverse semantic cues, resulting in a more comprehensive representation of native ad content. Second, by grounding its design in four implicit characteristics derived from prior literature, SENADA directly tackles the difficulty of distinguishing native ads from organic news, a challenge that has not been systematically addressed in earlier studies. This architecture demonstrates how task-specific encoders can be leveraged to enhance interpretability and classification performance in complex media content analysis.

Third, this research presents a newly developed annotated dataset in the Indonesian language, collected from six major electronic news portals. The dataset employs a multi-label annotation scheme to reflect the complex nature of native advertisements. Fourth, the proposed framework provides a practical solution for detecting native advertising in digital news content, especially in low-resource language settings such as Indonesian. This contribution supports efforts to promote media transparency and protect readers from misleading content. Finally, the approach offers a foundation for future research in multilingual native ads detection and explainable AI in news media analysis.

This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 discusses related works on native advertisement detection and deep learning ensemble techniques. Section 3 introduces the proposed framework, including the native advertisement characteristic representation and the BiLSTM architecture with an attention mechanism. Section 4 describes the dataset, experimental setup, and evaluation metrics used. Section 5 presents the experimental results, including comparative analysis with baseline models and state-of-the-art approaches. Finally, Section 6 provides conclusions and future research directions.

\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:related-works}
Wang \cite{b24} proposed an automatic Chinese text categorization method to address the classification of emergency event reports by leveraging the Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) for feature extraction. To tackle the class imbalance issue in emergency event categories, they introduced a novel loss function designed to enhance the performance of the BERT-based model. Additionally, the Adabound optimizer was employed to achieve a smooth transition from Adam to stochastic gradient descent, aiming to stabilize the learning process. The proposed method was evaluated on both imbalanced and balanced datasets and outperformed several benchmark models, including BERTLB, ABLG-CNN, TextRCNN, Att-BLSTM, and DPCNN, in terms of accuracy, weighted precision, recall, and F1-score. Sampling techniques such as random sampling, ADASYN, SMOTE, and Borderline-SMOTE were also applied to assess the effectiveness of the proposed loss function under imbalanced conditions, demonstrating its potential for real-world applications in smart emergency management systems.

Ghosh \cite{b25} developed an attention-based Bidirectional  LSTM–CNN model for detecting depressive content in Bangla social media texts. Recognizing the growing importance of mental health analysis via social platforms, the authors proposed a lightweight yet robust architecture combining LSTM and CNN's strengths with an attention mechanism to enhance interpretability and performance. A dedicated Bangla dataset was also constructed to support this task, addressing the lack of existing resources. The model achieved an accuracy of 94.3\%, with sensitivity and specificity values of 92.63\% and 95.12\%, respectively. Notably, the model also showed promising results when applied to English data. Compared to classical machine learning models, transformer-based approaches, and other neural architectures, the proposed model demonstrated superior performance and generalizability, making it a strong candidate for multilingual mental health detection systems.

Koru \cite{b14} addressed the challenge of fake news detection on Twitter, focusing on the Turkish language. They constructed a labeled dataset named TR\_FaRe\_News, which collects real news from verified newspaper accounts and fake news from fact-checking platforms. They used the Zemberek NLP tool tailored for Turkish to preprocess the texts. Before fine-tuning BERT-based models, the authors explored various machine learning and deep learning approaches, including ensemble methods, BoW, TF-IDF, and Word2Vec. They extended BERT with Bi-LSTM and CNN layers, experimenting with frozen and unfrozen parameter settings. They evaluated the models across seven benchmark datasets, including BuzzFeedNews, GossipCop, LIAR, and even an LLM-generated fake news dataset. Their best-performing models—BERTurk combined with CNN—achieved up to 94\% accuracy, highlighting the effectiveness of transformer-based architectures in low-resource language settings like Turkish.  Using information from the LIAR dataset, as referenced in \cite{b15}, Aslam proposed a machine learning model based on ensemble methods to categorize news stories as authentic or fraudulent.  A collection of attributes forms the basis of the LIAR dataset.

Thus, Aslam utilized two machine learning models. The dataset encompasses two categories of features: short textual elements, such as statements, and other elements, including speaker position, subject, and location. Several NLP methods are applied to the statement attribute, including tokenization, lemmatization, and removal of stop words. Following the introduction of the NLP technique, the subsequent step involves implementing deep learning to derive the model. The outputs of each model are subsequently processed through the ensemble voting method. The last step involves assessing the results. Malla classified the provided tweets into fake or real news categories, as noted in reference \cite{b16}. Malla tested several deep-learning models on a simulated COVID-19 dataset. In performance, deep learning models, CT-BERT and RoBERTa surpassed other deep learning models, including BERT, BERTweet, AlBERT, and DistilBERT. A proposed deep learning ensemble architecture surpassed CT-BERT and RoBERTa in performance on a fake COVID-19 news dataset through a multiplicative fusion technique.

Al-Yahya conducted a comprehensive comparative study \cite{b17} of neural network and transformer models employed in Arabic for detecting fake news. Notably, all models relied on vector embeddings of 100 dimensions. The linear models comprised two linear layers: a convolutional (Conv) layer, a batch normalization layer, and a ReLU activation function. The sequence model comprised a unidirectional layer with a hidden dimension of 100. A linear classification layer was appended to every model. Embeddings are a standard component found in the majority of model designs. Setting the embedding vector size to 100 is necessary to preserve the linearity of the model and align embeddings from various architectures. Isa \cite{b18} introduced IndoBERT, a transformer-based model built upon Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT), tailored for Indonesian and emphasizing context and attention within input sentences. IndoBERT comprises 12 hidden layers, with a single classification layer at the topmost position. Each layer applies multi-headed attention to the word embedding it receives from the preceding layer. The dimensions for each layer are specified as [number of tokens by 768 by batch size].
Following several experiments, Isa determined the optimal settings for her model, with the best result achieved at eight epochs, a batch size of 8 samples, and a learning rate of 2e-5. Isa further refined the proposed model by utilizing a dataset from turnbackhoax.id and optimized its hyperparameters to achieve optimal outcomes. Bensouda et al. \cite{b19} developed an ensemble model called BI-LSR by combining a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) network, a Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) classifier, and a Ridge Classifier using a blending technique. They trained and evaluated the model on real-world datasets, where it achieved an accuracy of 99.16\%. Their results show that blending deep learning with traditional classifiers can significantly improve fake news detection performance compared to individual models or previously proposed ensemble methods.

Text classification is a crucial function in natural language processing (NLP), which involves categorizing documents or written content into predetermined groups. Artificial neural networks with numerous layers have yielded impressive results in several disciplines, notably in text categorization. Ensemble learning methods, which combine multiple models to enhance performance, are effective in this task \cite{b20}. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) models are frequently employed in text classification tasks. Research indicates that BiLSTM surpasses more straightforward models like CNN primarily because it can process text in both forward and backward directions, leading to a more comprehensive contextual comprehension. The combination of CNN and BiLSTM with an attention mechanism achieved an accuracy 88.78\% in a text classification task as reported in reference \cite{b20}. This capability gives BiLSTM an advantage in various NLP applications. 

Prior research has contrasted the ensemble method with deep learning models that do not incorporate ensemble techniques. Implementing ensemble methods in deep learning architectures can significantly enhance their performance. Past research has employed BERT pre-training to identify fabricated news articles. Pretraining with BERT can enhance accuracy due to its high dimensionality, enabling it to capture the semantic meaning of the corpus effectively.

\section{Method}
The deep learning ensemble model has two main functions: as a model during training and testing. The training process functions to form a new model from the learning results carried out by the neural network through deep learning. This model is used for the native ads recognition process in the testing process. An overview of the stages of the deep learning ensemble model process developed can be seen in Figure \ref{fig:fig1}. This figure illustrates the workflow from raw input processing to final classification, emphasizing the role of sub-models in ensemble learning.

The training process has five main stages: preprocessing, word embedding, ensemble learning, combining streaming vector sentences, and forming a training model using deep learning, which is used to classify in the testing process. The initial stages of the testing process are almost the same as the training process. However, when entering ensemble learning and deep learning, classification is done by matching it with the training model formed in the training process. Furthermore, the output of the system will be produced, namely labeling whether the news content is "pure news" or "native ads." To support this classification, multiple sub-models are used to detect different linguistic cues indicative of advertising.

Model I through Model IV represent deep learning classifiers trained to detect specific characteristics within news content. Model I is responsible for detecting positive sentiment expressions commonly found in promotional text. Model II identifies persuasive language elements that suggest advertising intent, such as calls to action or implicit encouragements. Model III focuses on product or brand mentions within the text, indicating commercial relevance. Model IV is designed to analyze the news perspective, determining whether the content is presented from a singular, unbalanced viewpoint, which is typical in native advertising.

Each of these models processes the input independently and generates a vectorized representation, referred to as a streaming vector sentence. These vectors capture distinct semantic and structural signals derived from each classification objective. The vectors from all four models are then combined into a single feature representation through a feature concatenation step. 

This combined representation serves as the input to a BiLSTM model with an attention mechanism, which integrates contextual information across the entire sentence. The attention mechanism allows the model to weigh important parts of the input more heavily, enhancing classification accuracy.

Overall, the ensemble learning framework ensures that multiple aspects of native advertising—such as sentiment, persuasiveness, product references, and biased perspectives—are captured comprehensively. The final BiLSTM-based model leverages these enriched features to make a binary classification between "native ads" and "pure news." Compared to a single-model approach, this ensemble design provides greater robustness and interpretability. It also allows each sub-model to specialize in one aspect of the detection task, improving overall performance. The resulting architecture balances both feature diversity and deep contextual understanding, which are essential for accurate native ad identification.

\subsection{Ensemble Learning}
This study uses the concept of stacking ensemble learning. Stacking is one ensemble learning method that uses several base models by learning in parallel and independently on each base model and then combining them using a meta-learning algorithm to provide output from the results of the combination of base models. The study produces output in the form of streaming vector sentences. Streaming vector sentences from four models have much information related to the implicit characteristics of native ads. The implicit characteristics of native ads include four main aspects represented by the resulting vector patterns. First, native ads use positive or neutral sentiments reflected in vector patterns that show language that creates a good impression on readers. Second, content with persuasive properties is identified through vectors representing invitations or arguments to influence readers. Third, specific product or company promotions are captured in vectors that describe positive descriptions or focus on certain brands. Finally, the perspective of a single news source is reflected in the uniformity of vector patterns that indicate a lack of diversity of viewpoints. These four characteristics are combined in streaming vector sentences to reveal implicit patterns in native ads.

\subsection{Streaming Vector Sentence}
Traditional word embeddings assign fixed vectors to words without considering their context or position. In contrast, the Streaming Vector Sentence approach dynamically generates word representations by leveraging the position and context of each word within a sentence. As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:streaming-vector-sentence}, the input sentence "product x is offering a special promotion" is passed sequentially into deep learning models (Model I–IV), each trained for specific subtasks. Each word is transformed into a contextual vector representation, reflecting its semantic and syntactic role in the sentence. For example, the word “offering” may carry promotional intent, which the model captures by assigning higher weights to contextually relevant words like “promotion” and “special.” These vectors are generated by extracting the penultimate layer output of each model, ensuring that the semantic features are preserved without the final classification bias. The result is a dense, ordered vector sequence that can be further processed using BiLSTM and attention mechanisms to derive global sentence understanding. 

Streaming vector sentences is essential for tasks requiring fine-grained contextual comprehension, such as native ad or opinion detection in news articles. Standard word embeddings like Word2Vec or GloVe, while effective in capturing general semantic similarity, are context-independent and assign the same vector to a word regardless of its usage. This limitation is particularly problematic in news classification, where the meaning of a sentence often depends on subtle shifts in tone or intent. Streaming vector sentences overcome this by computing embeddings that are sensitive to their position and neighboring words. Additionally, because these vectors are generated using task-specific models, they implicitly encode features relevant to the classification goal (e.g., persuasiveness or positivity). This hybrid design—contextual, sequential, and model-aligned—makes the streaming vector sentence a superior choice for robust downstream processing.

\subsection{Feature Combination}
Feature combination in this study is the merging of streaming vector sentences used to combine information from several vectors representing different parts of a sentence or several sentences. This method is important to improve the accuracy of the NLP model by integrating information from various sources or parts of sentences. This study will try three methods of merging streaming vector sentences and choose the best one. The three methods for merging vectors are concatenate, add, and multiply.

\subsubsection{Concatenate}
The concatenate method combines vectors by adding them horizontally. With this method, the resulting new vector has dimensions that are the sum of the dimensions of the original vectors. For example, if you have two vectors, each with 100 dimensions, the result of concatenation will be a vector with 200 dimensions. The concatenate method generally keeps information from several vectors separately in the combined vector. Concatenate is helpful to ensure that all information is preserved without losing detail.

\subsection{Classification with Deep Learning}
Deep learning is a method that studies the hierarchy of features with the provision that the composition of lower-level features forms the hierarchy of features from a higher level. The concept of a neural network is described by representing more than one hidden layer to provide a deeper understanding of learning. Research on native ads detection on Indonesian news portals uses a deep learning model with the concept of a neural network. The learning method has two general stages, namely the training process and the testing process. The neural network used in the native ads detection model in this study uses an RNN basis so that, in general, information is processed sequentially with the addition of memory cells to remember word dependencies in long sentences called the BiLSTM model.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Dataset}
The development of datasets is one of the crucial aspects to support the analysis that needs to be carried out. We created our dataset for this study. The target was to collect at least 12,000 Indonesian-language electronic news \cite{b21}, in line with the amount of data contained in the LIAR dataset \cite{b22} with around 12,800 news items. This dataset was collected continuously until this dissertation was completed to ensure that the resulting dataset not only meets the required quantity but also reflects the current dynamics in electronic news. The news collected must meet specific criteria: each item consists of at least 100 words and only includes text content, excluding images or news titles. We addressed the issue of class imbalance and minimized bias toward the majority class by ensuring a balanced dataset, with 50\% of the instances labeled as native advertisements and the remaining 50\% as regular news articles. We ensured the reliability of the dataset labeling process byinvolving three independent annotators with backgrounds in media and linguistics, acknowledging the subjective nature of identifying native advertisements. To quantify the consistency of labeling among annotators, Fleiss’ Kappa was computed since more than two raters were involved. The agreement score reached 0.90, indicating substantial agreement. \subsection{Experimental Setup}
In this study, all models were implemented using the Keras library \cite{b23}, which provides a high-level API interface for building and training neural networks. The designed model aims to combine four input feature representations derived from different data: positive vector, persuasive vector, product vector, and perspective vector. A separate input layer with dimensions represents each input feature according to each data representation. The four features are combined using the Concatenate layer, and additional dimensions are added using the Lambda layer to ensure dimensional compatibility with the next layer.

The model architecture uses the BiLSTM approach with two layers. The first layer has 256 units and produces output in sequences, while the second layer also produces 256 units but only produces the final output. After the LSTM layer, a Dropout layer with a level of 0.5 is applied to prevent overfitting. The attention mechanism is implemented with a softmax-activated Dense layer, which is recombined with the previous output to increase focus on important features. The output layer is Dense with sigmoid activation used to predict the probability in two classes. The training parameters include a batch size of 256 and a maximum number of epochs of 50. 


\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Data Processing and Generating Streaming Vector Sentences}
In the initial implementation stage, the data that has gone through the process of detecting the implicit characteristics of native ads is further processed using four different deep learning models, each selected based on the best performance they offer in a particular task. Positive News Detection Model with CNN: This model uses CNN, which has been optimized to detect positive sentiment in news. CNN was chosen because of its ability to capture spatial text features, which is important for identifying positive patterns in news content. The output of this model is a streaming vector sentence that represents the level of positivity in the news. Persuasive News Detection Model with BiGRU: BiGRU is used to detect persuasive elements in news text. BiGRU was chosen because of its ability to capture temporal dependencies in both directions (forward and backward), which is important in understanding the persuasive context. This model produces streaming vector sentences containing information about the news's persuasive elements.

Product/Company News Detection Model with BiLSTM: To detect references to products or companies in the news, the BiLSTM model is used. BiLSTM was chosen because of its ability to handle long and complex data sequences, such as product or company names, that may appear in multiple contexts. Streaming vector sentences from this model represent the presence and relevance of the product or company in the news. News Perspective Detection Model with BiLSTM: This model uses BiLSTM to detect perspectives or biases that may be present in news presentations. By capturing recurring patterns and sentiments in the text, BiLSTM helps identify the news narrative's perspectives. The output of this model is streaming vector sentences that describe the perspectives or biases in the news content. Each model produces streaming vector sentences combined using different merging methods, such as concatenate, add, and multiply, to determine the most effective combination in detecting native ads.

\subsection{Training Process Using BiLSTM}
After obtaining the streaming vector sentences from the previous process, the next step is to combine the vectors using three different concatenation methods: concatenate, add, and multiply. The concatenation of these vectors is very important because it can affect how information from different models is combined and processed by the BiLSTM model. These concatenation methods were tested to determine which performed best in detecting native ads. The first concatenation method is concatenate. This method combines the vectors from positive news detection, persuasive detection, product/company detection, and perspective detection models horizontally. The concatenation method combines these vectors into one long vector that combines information from all models. The resulting concatenation vector is then expanded in dimension to suit the input needs of the BiLSTM model.

The second concatenation method is added. This method combines the vectors by summing the corresponding elements of each vector. By summing these vectors, the information from each model is combined to better facilitate learning more complex patterns. The result of this method is a single vector that reflects the combined information from all models in a different way than the concatenation method. The third concatenation method is multiply. In this method, vectors are combined by multiplying the corresponding elements of each vector. This method can help emphasize features that frequently occur together in multiple vectors and reduce the influence of features that rarely occur. The result of the multiply method is a single vector that combines information in a way that may be more sensitive to interactions between different features.

After implementing these three merging methods, an evaluation was conducted to determine which method performed best in the context of the data used. This evaluation included measuring accuracy and other metrics for each method. The best performance method was selected for use in the following training process. The processed and merged data and the representations generated from the best merging method were then used to train a BiLSTM model with an additional attention mechanism. The BiLSTM model was chosen because of its superior ability to capture sequential patterns and temporal relationships in text data, which are very important for native ad detection. The attention mechanism was added to allow the model to give more weight to important parts of the text input.

The training process was carried out with optimal hyperparameter settings, such as the number of epochs, batch size, and learning rate. These settings were designed to ensure that the model learned well without overfitting. Regularization techniques such as dropout are applied to reduce overfitting. The BiLSTM model can effectively utilize information from multiple sources and provide accurate results in detecting native ads. Evaluation of the trained model will provide insights into the effectiveness of the selected vector fusion method and the model's ability to overcome the challenges faced in native ad detection.

A deeper analysis reveals several key reasons behind these errors. First, the distribution of the four native ad characteristics is not perfectly balanced. Second, confusion matrix results indicate that the model struggles particularly with articles that are subtly persuasive or present only one side of a story without strong sentiment. These cases often resemble regular news in structure but implicitly promote a product or viewpoint, making them difficult to detect. Finally, linguistic ambiguity in the text contributes to the classification challenge. Many articles use vague or neutral language, causing the model to miss cues that might be obvious to human readers. As a potential solution, future work could explore soft labeling or introduce an "ambiguous" label category to better capture these borderline cases.

Compared to BiLSTM (2.17 seconds) and BiGRU (5.64 seconds), SENADA’s optimized design demonstrates better responsiveness while preserving high accuracy. In addition, since SENADA employs multiple deep learning encoders for feature extraction, it is important to consider the full inference cost and latency when applied in real-world environments. Although the model shows efficient inference time in controlled evaluation, practical deployment may require further system-level latency and scalability analysis. The attention mechanism helps improve interpretability by identifying key parts of the text, but more work is needed to ensure that predictions can be meaningfully explained to end users and stakeholders, especially in media or regulatory contexts.

In contrast, BiLSTM and BiGRU, despite achieving better accuracy than CNN, exhibit significantly higher training and inference times. These findings highlight the trade-off between computational efficiency and model performance, reinforcing the importance of optimizing ensemble methods for accuracy and efficiency in native ad classification tasks. Future research can explore further optimizations such as model pruning, knowledge distillation, or adaptive inference strategies to improve the computational efficiency of the SENADA model without sacrificing classification accuracy.

\subsubsection{Comparison with LLM}
To further evaluate the performance of the SENADA model, a comparative analysis was conducted against a large language model, Gemma, using BERTScore as the evaluation metric. BERTScore is particularly suitable for the semantic-level comparison, as it aligns model-generated text with reference outputs based on contextual embeddings from pre-trained language models. The evaluation shows that Gemma achieved a BERTScore Precision of 0.9293, Recall of 0.9289, and F1 Score of 0.9289, indicating high semantic similarity in text classification tasks. These results reflect the capacity of LLMs to capture nuanced language patterns and deep contextual representations. However, evaluating performance solely based on semantic similarity does not always reflect the model’s effectiveness in identifying implicit promotional intent, which is central to native ad detection.

In contrast, the SENADA model also demonstrates strong semantic alignment, with BERTScore results on par with Gemma’s, particularly in terms of Recall and Precision. SENADA achieves an F1 Score of 0.93 across traditional metrics and maintains semantic robustness, confirming its effectiveness in understanding surface-level and latent features of native advertisements. Unlike Gemma, which is pre-trained on general-purpose corpora, SENADA is fine-tuned specifically for the native ad classification task, allowing it to better differentiate between organic content and promotional narratives. This domain-specific fine-tuning is critical in enhancing classification performance beyond general language understanding. Thus, SENADA combines task-specific optimization and semantic sensitivity, resulting in a competitive advantage over generic LLMs.

From a resource efficiency perspective, SENADA also offers significant advantages. Gemma requires extensive computational resources for training and inference as a large-scale transformer model, which can pose challenges for deployment in real-world scenarios with limited infrastructure. In contrast, SENADA is built on a more compact and efficient ensemble architecture, integrating BiLSTM with attention mechanisms and feature concatenation strategies. This architecture enables high performance without incurring the computational overhead typical of LLMs. Therefore, SENADA offers a practical and effective solution for institutions aiming to deploy native ad detection systems in production environments.

Overall, the comparative analysis shows that while LLMs like Gemma demonstrate strong general language capabilities, task-specific models such as SENADA can match or even surpass their performance in focused classification tasks. The findings highlight that when designed with task relevance in mind, fine-tuned deep learning architectures remain highly competitive even in the presence of state-of-the-art LLMs. Furthermore, SENADA’s consistent performance across traditional and semantic metrics reinforces its robustness and reliability. These results suggest that future research may benefit from combining the scalability of LLMs with the precision of specialized architectures. The synergy between general-purpose understanding and domain-specific adaptation could yield even more effective approaches to native advertisement detection.

\subsection{Discussion and Limitations}
In addition to its strong performance, this study contributes significantly by introducing a benchmark dataset for native advertisement detection in the Indonesian language. This area has been largely underexplored in previous research. The proposed SENADA architecture offers not only high accuracy but also interpretability, making it applicable to other low-resource languages and domains where implicit promotional content resembles editorial writing. With further refinement and scalability, this framework has the potential to support media transparency and automated monitoring systems on a broader scale. 

Despite its strengths, the proposed model faces several limitations that should be addressed in future work. One key challenge lies in the implicit nature of native ads, where only a small portion of the article may reflect native advertising characteristics. At the same time, the rest mimics standard news writing. This uneven signal distribution makes detection more difficult, as relevant cues are sparse and context-dependent. However, the data set constructed in this study has shown strong representativeness and quality, allowing effective model training.

Furthermore, the results of the evaluation indicate that characteristics such as positive sentiment and persuasive tone play an important role in the successful classification. Future research may further explore these aspects by using more advanced semantic modeling techniques. For instance, implementing a specialized multi-head attention mechanism to weigh each native ad trait—sentiment, persuasion, product mention, and perspective could enhance performance and provide deeper interpretability.

The general architecture of SENADA is language agnostic, as it builds on pre-trained multilingual BERT embeddings and a modular classification structure. This design enables adaptation to other languages by retraining on annotated datasets with similar native advertisement characteristics. As long as native ads in the target language exhibit comparable implicit features such as persuasive tone, biased perspective, or product mentions, the SENADA framework can be fine-tuned effectively. Future studies could validate this by applying the model to multilingual corpora and assessing cross-lingual transfer capabilities, which would expand its relevance beyond the Indonesian context.

While this study demonstrates strong model performance through standard evaluation metrics, one important limitation is the lack of human validation involving professionals such as editors or journalists. The current evaluation does not capture how these experts perceive or interpret the model's predictions. Given the subjective and contextual nature of native advertising, future research should incorporate qualitative validation by domain experts. Involving media professionals in evaluating model outputs can provide deeper insights into the classifications' practical applicability, perceived reliability, and editorial relevance. Their feedback could also guide model refinement to align with journalistic standards and ethical considerations in news publishing.

Although the SENADA model achieves competitive results, its ensemble-based structure introduces complexity and an extended training time. To mitigate this, future efforts may consider adopting multitask learning (MTL) to learn all native ad characteristics within a unified model jointly. This approach may improve efficiency and generalization. Furthermore, knowledge distillation or lightweight ensemble strategies could simplify the architecture while maintaining model performance, enabling easier deployment in real-world applications.

\section{Conclusion}
This study presents a comprehensive approach to detecting native advertisements in Indonesian electronic news by introducing a high-quality dataset and a novel deep learning architecture. A dedicated dat set was developed by collecting and annotating news articles from six major online news portals. The annotation process focused on four implicit characteristics of native ads: positive or neutral sentiment, promotional content, single-point-of-view reporting, and persuasive writing style. Manual labeling was performed by trained annotators and validated by domain experts, following a strict codebook and inter-annotator agreement process. This dataset fills a significant gap in Indonesian NLP resources and provides a solid foundation for future research in native ad detection. 

To explore the effectiveness of various deep learning models, the study evaluated BiGRU, BiLSTM, and CNN for multiple classification tasks related to native ad features. The BiGRU model consistently outperformed others in detecting positive sentiment and persuasive content, while BiLSTM demonstrated superior performance in identifying product/company mentions and narrative perspective. CNN showed relatively high overall accuracy but struggled in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score, indicating a tendency toward misclassification in tasks requiring fine-grained contextual understanding. 

The research further introduced SENADA, a custom ensemble architecture combining BERT embeddings, ensemble learning strategies, and a BiLSTM model with an attention mechanism. The approach incorporates sentence-level streaming vectors with various fusion strategies—concatenate, add, and multiply. Among these, the concatenate method yielded the best results, achieving 93\% across accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The attention mechanism significantly enhanced the model’s ability to capture contextual relationships and distinguish subtle linguistic patterns typical of native ads. 

Despite the promising results, this study is not without limitations. While carefully constructed, the dataset may still carry annotation bias due to the subjective nature of interpreting implicit advertisement features. Moreover, certain native ad traits can be highly context-dependent and culturally nuanced, which poses additional challenges in ensuring consistency and objectivity. Another limitation lies in the class distribution across the annotated labels, which, although balanced during preprocessing, may not reflect the natural frequency of such ads in real-world data, potentially affecting model generalizability.

In addition, the linguistic complexity of Bahasa, including the use of informal expressions, idioms, and regional variations, introduces challenges for semantic representation and contextual modeling, particularly for transformer-based models pre-trained primarily on general-domain or English data. Future work should explore more robust pretraining strategies, bias mitigation, and evaluation across larger and more diverse corpora to improve scalability and fairness in real-world applications.

Overall, the proposed method has proven effective in detecting native ads that often disguise themselves as regular editorial content. The combination of linguistic insight, attention-based modeling, and ensemble learning enables the system to perform consistently across various textual conditions. This work not only contributes a new benchmark dataset for the Indonesian language but also offers a scalable and interpretable architecture for native ad detection, which can be adapted for broader applications in media analysis and digital content regulation.
