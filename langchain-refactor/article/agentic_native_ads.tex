\begin{abstract}
The rise of native advertising, which blends promotional content with editorial news, poses a significant threat to digital media credibility and user trust. While traditional deep learning models have shown promise in detecting such content, they often lack the transparency and explainability required for newsroom applications. This study introduces SENADA 2.0, an advanced agentic AI framework designed for the automated detection and explanation of native advertisements in Indonesian digital news portals. Departing from static ensemble architectures, SENADA 2.0 utilizes a modular pipeline comprising five specialized agents: Web Scraper, Preprocessing, Retriever, Classifier, and Explainer. By leveraging Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Large Language Models (LLMs), the framework not only identifies native advertisements with high accuracy but also provides human-readable justifications for its classifications. Experimental evaluations on a large-scale Indonesian news dataset demonstrate that the agentic approach achieves state-of-the-art results while significantly improving model interpretability. This framework offers a robust and transparent solution for media monitors and news consumers, bridging the gap between automated detection and human-centric reasoning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
The digital media landscape has undergone a profound transformation with the rise of hybrid content, most notably in the form of native advertising \cite{b1}. Marketers increasingly favor native advertisements because they blend seamlessly into the editorial flow, effectively overcoming the phenomenon of banner blindness \cite{b2}. This non-disruptive nature allows promotional content to achieve significantly higher engagement rates compared to traditional display formats \cite{b3}. However, the integration of commercial messaging within electronic news portals complicates the distinction between independent journalism and paid promotion \cite{b4}. Consequently, the rapid global expansion of these advertisements has created an urgent need for advanced detection systems that can protect digital media integrity.

In the context of Indonesian digital journalism, the proliferation of native ads without clear disclosures has raised serious ethical concerns. These advertisements often mimic the structure and tone of organic news so closely that they can inadvertently mislead the general public \cite{b5}. Such concealment of commercial intent poses a direct threat to the perceived credibility of established news institutions \cite{b6}. Readers are entitled to transparency regarding the origin and purpose of the information they consume in the digital sphere \cite{b7}. Therefore, addressing the deceptive potential of these advertisements is essential for maintaining trust between media providers and their audiences.

Detecting native advertisements is an inherently challenging task due to their sophisticated linguistic mimicry of editorial styles. Unlike fake news or propaganda which often contain factual inaccuracies, native ads are strategically biased yet factually grounded \cite{b8}. Their implicit nature involves a favorable portrayal of brands through subtle persuasive cues and one-sided perspectives \cite{b9}. Identifying these patterns manually is nearly impossible given the massive volume of news generated by online portals daily \cite{b10}. Thus, developing robust computational frameworks is a critical requirement for scaling media monitoring efforts in complex linguistic environments.

Previous attempts to automate the detection of these advertisements focused on various machine learning and deep learning architectures. Models such as the original SENADA framework utilized stacked ensemble learning to capture implicit traits like positive sentiment and promotional focus \cite{b21}. While these conventional deep learning approaches achieved high classification accuracy, they predominantly functioned as static "black-box" systems \cite{b20}. These models often fail to provide the transparency required for institutional applications where the rationale behind a decision is paramount. Furthermore, the rigidity of traditional architectures limits their adaptability to the evolving tactics used in modern native advertising.

The lack of interpretability in automated systems represents a significant gap that must be bridged for editorial adoption. Media professionals and news consumers need to understand the evidence-based reasoning that leads to a specific classification decision. Simply providing a binary label is insufficient for building trust in automated media monitoring tools. There is a growing demand for "Explainable AI" that can articulate linguistic findings in a human-readable format. Addressing this need requires a paradigm shift from purely predictive models toward systems that prioritize both performance and transparency.

To address these evolving challenges, this study introduces an innovative framework based on the concept of Agentic AI. SENADA 2.0 utilizes modular agentic workflows orchestrated through Large Language Models to simulate complex human reasoning. This approach decomposes the detection task into a collaborative effort among specialized autonomous agents. By integrating Retrieval-Augmented Generation, the system anchors its classifications in a vast knowledge base of verified news content. This synergistic design enables the model to leverage both general linguistic knowledge and specific domain-relevant contextual data.

The proposed SENADA 2.0 framework implements a five-agent pipeline consisting of scraping, preprocessing, retrieval, classification, and explanation modules. Each agent is designed to handle a specific stage of the analysis, ensuring high modularity and ease of optimization. The system specifically targets the four implicit characteristics of native ads through sophisticated prompt engineering and tool integration. This specialized architecture significantly improves upon previous ensemble methods by offering real-time analysis and detailed justifications. By combining task separation with advanced reasoning engines, SENADA 2.0 provides a robust solution for automated native ad detection.

Section III describes the large-scale Indonesian news dataset and the multi-label annotation process used for grounding. Section IV presents the detailed architecture of the agentic pipeline and the orchestration logic employed. \section{Related Works}
\label{sec:related-works}
Automated text classification in the news domain has long been a central focus of natural language processing (NLP). Early research primarily focused on topic categorization and sentiment analysis. However, as deceptive content became more prevalent, the focus shifted toward identifying misinformation, propaganda, and more recently, native advertising.

Early methods for native ad detection relied on lexical analysis and structural features, such as the ratio of capitalized words, sentence length, and the presence of specific advertising keywords. These classical machine learning approaches, using algorithms like SVM and Random Forest, provided a baseline but were easily bypassed by the natural, editorial-style language used in modern native advertising. The introduction of word embeddings like Word2Vec and GloVe allowed for a better capture of semantic relationships, leading to the use of deep learning models.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) were initially favored for their ability to detect local features and patterns within sentences, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), specifically LSTMs and GRUs, proved effective in modeling the sequential nature of news text \cite{b20}. The original SENADA framework \cite{b21} significantly improved upon these methods by using a stacked ensemble approach. By training independent sub-models to detect specific traits (e.g., persuasiveness vs. product mentions) and then combining their vector representations, SENADA was able to capture the multi-dimensional nature of native advertisements more effectively than single-model architectures.

Despite these successes, the field has been disrupted by the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs). Transformers, such as BERT and its successors (RoBERTa, T5, GPT), have set new benchmarks for text understanding by utilizing multi-headed attention mechanisms to capture bidirectional context. Studies have shown that BERT-based models significantly outperform sequential models like BiLSTM in detecting subtle linguistic cues \cite{b8}, \cite{b9}. However, using LLMs for native ad detection still faces challenges related to "hallucinations" and the lack of grounding in specific domains.

Current research is moving toward "Agentic AI" and "Agentic Workflows" to overcome these limitations. Frameworks such as LangChain and AutoGPT enable the creation of multi-agent systems where LLMs act as the core reasoning engine, but are supported by specialized tools and agents for information retrieval and data processing. While agentic frameworks have shown promise in general-purpose software development and scientific research, their application in the critical field of media monitoring and native ad detection remains a nascent area. SENADA 2.0 represents the first comprehensive implementation of an agentic framework dedicated specifically to the Indonesian news ecosystem, integrating RAG to ensure that classification is grounded in verified contextual data.

\section{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}
The foundation of this study is an extensive dataset of Indonesian-language electronic news articles, specifically curated for native advertisement detection. The dataset comprises over 12,000 articles collected from six major Indonesian news portals (e.g., Kompas, Detik, Tempo) between February and August 2022. This collection covers diverse categories including Economy, Lifestyle, Automotive, and Technology.

A critical aspect of this dataset is its multi-label annotation. Each article was manually annotated by three independent experts based on four implicit characteristics: (1) positive or neutral sentiment, (2) persuasive tone, (3) promotional focus, and (4) single-source perspective. The inter-annotator agreement, measured via Fleiss' Kappa, reached 0.90, indicating high reliability. In SENADA 2.0, this dataset serves a dual purpose: it was used for fine-tuning the underlying Large Language Models (LLMs) and acts as the ground-truth knowledge base for the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) component, stored in a high-dimensional vector space for real-time context retrieval.

\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:methods}
The proposed SENADA 2.0 framework is designed as a modular agentic pipeline. The architecture is built on the principle of task separation, where each stage of the detection and explanation process is handled by a specialized agent. This modularity allows for easier updates, better error handling, and the ability to use the most suitable model for each specific task.

\subsection{Agentic Workflow and Orchestration}
Unlike traditional linear pipelines, SENADA 2.0 employs an "Agentic Workflow" orchestrated using the LangChain framework. This workflow is not merely a sequence of steps but a collaborative process where agents can pass structured data, metadata, and reasoning traces to one another. The central orchestrator manages the state and history of the detection task, ensuring that the Classifier Agent has access to the raw content from the Scraper, the extracted features from the Preprocessor, and the semantic context from the Retriever.

\subsection{Detailed Agent Architecture}
The framework is decomposed into five specialized agents:

\subsubsection{Web Scraper Agent}
The extraction process uses \texttt{WebScraperTool} and \texttt{SeleniumScraperTool}. This agent is responsible for navigating complex HTML structures and rendering JavaScript-heavy pages to retrieve the full article content. It handles edge cases such as infinite scrolling and dynamic pop-ups, which are common in modern news portals.

\subsubsection{Preprocessing Agent}
This agent uses a suite of tools including \texttt{TextCleanerTool} and \texttt{FeatureExtractorTool}. It leverages regex-based patterns and NLTK for tokenization, but also performs advanced feature extraction, identifying specific "trigger words" and syntactic patterns associated with persuasive writing. This pre-extracted information is provided as auxiliary input to the LLM to focus its attention on promotional markers.

\subsubsection{Retriever Agent}
The Retriever Agent implements an advanced RAG pipeline. It utilizes \texttt{NativeAdsVectorStore}, built using FAISS and \texttt{sentence-transformers} (specifically the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} model). When a new article is processed, this agent retrieves top-K similar examples from the 12,000-article dataset. These examples include both verified native ads and legitimate news, providing the Classifier Agent with "contextual anchors" to perform comparative reasoning.

\subsubsection{Classifier Agent}
This agent serves as the core reasoning engine. It uses a fine-tuned LLM optimized for the Indonesian news domain. The input to this agent is a multi-part prompt containing: (1) the article text, (2) pre-extracted linguistic features, and (3) retrieved contextual examples. The agent is instructed to analyze the content against the four theoretical characteristics of native ads and output a classification label with a confidence score.

\subsubsection{Explanation Agent}
The Explanation Agent takes the raw reasoning trace from the Classifier and transforms it into a structured, human-readable explanation. It maps the LLM's findings back to specific portions of the text, highlighting exact sentences that exhibit promotional bias or persuasive intent. This results in a "white-box" output that justifies the classification to the user.
